Questions

Things people ask before they call.

I just got a letter from the IRS. What should I do first?

Don’t ignore it, and don’t pay it just because it arrived. Most notices have a response deadline, and most of them can be answered. Send me the notice number — it’s in the top right corner, something like CP2000 or CP504 — and I’ll tell you what it actually means and what your options are.

What is an Enrolled Agent, and why does it matter?

An Enrolled Agent is a tax professional licensed directly by the IRS, after passing a three-part exam covering individual tax, business tax, and representation. It’s a federal credential, so it’s valid in all fifty states.

It matters because only Enrolled Agents, CPAs, and attorneys can represent you before the IRS without limits. Most tax preparers can file your return but cannot argue your case.

I haven’t filed in years. Am I in serious trouble?

Usually less than you think. Unfiled years are the most common problem I see and often the most fixable — when you don’t file, the IRS can file for you using all of your income and none of your deductions, which produces a balance far higher than what’s really owed. Filing the real returns frequently reduces it substantially, and sometimes eliminates it.

Can you help if I don’t live in California?

Yes. The Enrolled Agent credential is federal, so I can represent taxpayers in every state. I work with clients across the country by phone, video, and secure portal. Everything can be handled remotely.

Will I have to talk to the IRS myself?

No. Once you sign a power of attorney, I become the point of contact. Their correspondence comes to me, I make the calls, and I attend the audit if there is one. You’ll hear what’s happening from me, in plain language.

How much will it cost?

Preparation prices are published above. Relief work is quoted as a flat fee after a free review of your transcripts, because the honest answer depends on what your case actually involves. You’ll always know the full price before any work begins, and it doesn’t change afterward.

How do I know my information is safe?

Documents move through an encrypted client portal, never email. I keep a Written Information Security Plan on file as the IRS requires of paid preparers, and your records stay encrypted both in storage and in transit.

What do you need from me to get started?

For a free review, just any notices you’ve received and permission to pull your IRS transcripts. That’s it. I’ll have a much clearer picture after reading the record than either of us can get from guessing.
